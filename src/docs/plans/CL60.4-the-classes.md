## Context

CL60.2 and CL60.3 turned every flowchart and sequence into a picture. The docs' last Mermaid is 11 `classDiagram`
blocks: one in the evals' architecture and ten in plans. They use `class X { … }` with members and a
`<<stereotype>>`, and four kinds of relation with a label (`-->`, `..>`, `o--`, `<|--` and `<|..`); the biggest has
31 types and 24 relations. This child adds a `classes` kind, swaps the 11 blocks, rewrites the plans' rule so every
diagram is a picture, holds it with a test that no doc holds Mermaid at all, and records the rule in an ADR.
CL60.5 then uses the same kind for the interface diagram on every project doc.

## Design

### What changes, in plain words

- **A class chart is data:** its types (a name, a kind such as `interface` or `record`, its members, an icon and a
  tone) and its relations (from, to, a kind, an optional word).
- **It is drawn as a flow.** The `classes` kind turns each type into a flow box, its name and kind on top and its
  members under a divider, and each relation into an arrow: `inherits` and `implements` dashed and named so, `holds`
  solid, `uses` and `creates` dashed with their word. The flows' layout, router, tones, chips and frame do the rest.
- **A flow box may list members**, in the code font under a divider, the box as wide as its longest member.
- **The import** reads the docs' class blocks, `~T~` generics back to `<T>`.
- **The rule:** every diagram in a doc is a JSON our renderer draws; no Mermaid anywhere, held by a test and an ADR.

## Steps

One commit each, test first, seen failing.

1. **This plan**, its four pictures drawn.
2. **The class source.** `DiagramKind.Classes`, the spec records; the reader refuses a relation naming a type the
   chart lacks. Tests: read, refused, written back the same.
3. **Members in a flow box.** `GraphNode.Members`: the box wide enough for its longest member and tall enough for
   all, the members under a divider. Tests with numbers; the site's goldens unchanged.
4. **The classes renderer.** `ClassFlow.Of` turns types into boxes and relations into arrows with their style and
   word; `ClassesRenderer` draws it through the flow. Tests: each relation kind's style and word; a golden.
5. **The import.** `MermaidClassReader` reads the forms above; `import` writes all three kinds. Tests on samples.
6. **The 11 class charts, and no Mermaid left.** A test that no doc holds a Mermaid block of any kind, seen failing
   on the 11; each imported, toned, looked at and swapped; the flowchart and sequence tests fold into it.
7. **The rule.** The plans README asks for all four diagrams as pictures; an ADR records that a diagram is data our
   renderer draws and no doc holds Mermaid.
8. **The docs.** The how-to's class section, the project doc, the work log, this plan deleted.

## Approved during the build

Each finding below was settled unattended (2026-11-12), the recommended of three options.

- **A type takes its kind's tone** (step 4's fix): a type with no tone named is teal when its kind says interface,
  record or enum (contracts and state), green otherwise (this project). The other options: everything plain, which
  leaves the charts grey, or a tone written on every type, which is 200 hand edits.
- **A long member wraps at 60 characters**, its later lines indented (step 4's fix), so one signature can't make a
  box 1,000 px wide. The other options: cut it with an ellipsis, which loses the signature, or no limit.
- **A shared word is said once** (step 4's fixes): when several arrows into one type, or out of one type, carry the
  same word ("implements", "holds"), only the first shows its chip. The fan-ins had a pile of identical chips. The
  other options: no words on implements arrows (style only, Q3's rejected option), or chips moved apart, which is a
  router change.
- **The evals' nine check classes are one type** ("The checks", nine, each its own class) with one implements arrow:
  nine boxes that only repeat ICheck made the chart 3,000 px tall. The other options: keep the nine, or leave them
  out and lose that they exist.
- **F39's and F40's charts are split in two** (step 6): F39's 31 types became part A (the engine) and part B (the
  triage service, part A's four pieces in violet), F40's became the agent's side and the evals' side. The whole
  charts were tangles of crossing arrows (2,300 and 2,970 px). The other options: keep them as plan charts that are
  deleted when done, or teach the layout to cross fewer lines, which no plan names.
- **Mermaid's own notation is cleaned by hand in the data** (step 6): the reader keeps a member's text as written,
  so F125's, F131's and F134's `%%` comments became words in brackets, and F126's `;` separators and `$` static
  mark went (`(static)`). The other options: teach the reader those marks, for an import that has no Mermaid left
  to read, or leave them showing.
- **Wide charts stay**: F106.1 (2,047 px), F92 (2,191 px) and F40's agent side (2,293 px) read well at their width,
  and the plans are deleted when their work is done.
