# F134.9: The cards picture, redone

> **Plan.** A child of [F134](F134-the-showroom-looks-modern.md): the `cards` picture, the overview every project
> doc opens with, drawn in a look of its own. The project is a headline on a light, and its parts hang from a lit
> rail under it.

**Status:** approved 2026-11-14 (Louis: "go"). **Branch:** `feature/F134.1-the-start-is-a-stage`,
where F134.7 and F134.8 were built. **Design:** [m-cards.html](F134-mockups/m-cards.html), opened in a browser
from the repo.

## Context

Louis, 2026-11-14, with a screenshot of "louis-agent.web at a glance": "this is the next one we are going to redo,
this one must be super fancy he can follow a diff style, keep it like dark tones but may introduce limited colors
and also fancy." Three designs were drawn from that picture's real data, and he picked **A, "Marquee"**: one
accent, the project as a headline on a spotlight, the parts on glass cards hung from a glowing rail.

**What was looked up.**

- *The code.* `CardLayout.Place` puts the rows top to bottom, a row's cards sharing its width, every word in the
  code font so a line's width is its length times one number. `CardsRenderer` draws one frame, the links as thin
  lines with arrowheads, and each card by its look. The docs' frame, `TitledFrame`, adds a title bar and a note
  bar. A card holds an id, an icon, a title, an optional code name, a line of text and a look (`plain`,
  `highlight`, `heading`). It has no tone, so every colour in the new look is the design's own.
- *The pictures.* 45 use this kind. 39 are the mockup's shape: one highlighted card on top that feeds every card
  of the row under it. Six are not:

  | Picture | What it has |
  |---|---|
  | the triage identity's overview | arrows both ways, to four cards |
  | the general packs' overview | no links at all, three rows |
  | the site pack's overview | one more link, from a part to a card under it |
  | ADR 0027's figure | no highlighted card, arrows both ways |
  | the architecture's building blocks | a heading on top, the highlighted engine in row three, two cards feeding one |
  | F148's layers | six rows in a chain, a heading at the bottom |

- *The links.* All 139 join two neighbouring rows. None skips a row and none stays inside one row.
- *The words.* The longest title of a highlighted card is 36 characters, its longest text 107, its longest code
  name 32. A part's longest title is 35 and its longest text 116. Three of the 45 have a caption.
- *Who else draws one.* The chat's `DrawnCard` draws whatever picture an agent wrote through the same renderer,
  so a cards picture there gets the new look with no work of its own. No page of a site declares one.
- *The rules that will run.* `FolderSizeTests` allows six source files a folder: `Svg/Renderers` and
  `Svg/Renderers/Parts` each hold six today, so the new drawing gets a folder of its own. `FileSizeTests` allows
  200 lines of code a file. `DocsDiagramsTests` holds each of the 45 SVGs to its JSON, byte for byte.
- *The pattern.* The rail is the org chart's connector, which graph tools call bus-style routing: a parent's line
  drops to a bar its children hang from. Stated from memory; no source was re-read for this plan.

**His two picks before the plan, each of three options (2026-11-14).**

- *How far the rail goes:* "A rail for each sender". Between two rows, every card that sends links gets its own
  rail over the cards it feeds, and two cards feeding one share a rail above it.
- *What a highlighted card is when it is not on top, and what a heading is:* "The look decides, anywhere". A
  highlighted card is the headline on its light wherever it stands, and the light follows it. A heading is a
  quieter headline with no light. A picture with no highlighted card has no light.

**What could not be confirmed.** How the light looks on a light page, and how two rails in one gap read, are not
drawn yet. Step 2 draws both, and the plan stops there until Louis has seen them.

## Design

**The picture, part by part.**

- *Its own title.* The picture's title stands small in its top left corner, and the docs' frame gives a cards
  picture only its note bar, as it does a flow. The mockup drew "at a glance" in a quieter tone than the name
  before it. That would need a rule about those three words, so the title is drawn in one tone.
- *A highlighted card is a headline.* No box: an icon in an accent badge, the title large, the code name in a pill
  under it, the text under that, all centred, on a soft light in the accent. It stands wherever its row is.
- *A heading is a quieter headline.* The same words, smaller, with a plain icon and no light.
- *A plain card is glass.* A faint panel with a hairline, an accent line along its top, its icon in a tinted
  badge beside its title, and its text under them.
- *A card no link touches is quiet*, in a picture that has links: no accent line and no badge. In a picture with
  no links at all, every plain card is drawn as glass, since none is more loose than another.
- *A rail.* Between two neighbouring rows, the cards above that feed the same cards below share one rail. A stem
  runs from each card above down to the rail, and a drop from the rail down to each card below. Two rails in one
  gap whose spans are clear of each other stand at one height, and one that would lie on another stands a step
  under it. The gap is as tall as its rails need.
- *Which way a link points.* A small arrowhead ends a drop at the card below for `down`, a stem at the card above
  for `up`, and both for `both`. Dots run along the line the same way, and stand still with motion off, so the
  direction never rests on motion alone.
- *A link the rail cannot draw.* A link that skips a row, or joins two cards of one row, is refused when the JSON
  is read, with the reason. No picture has one, and nothing is built for a case no picture has (ADR 0055).
- *The colour.* One accent, from violet to blue, taken from the tones the palette already has in both schemes.
  The picture carries a light and a dark look, as every picture does.
- *The words fit by a generous measure.* The new look sets its words in the sans font, as a flow's boxes are. A
  line's width is then taken as its length times a number chosen wide, and every picture is looked at. That is
  the trade a flow already made.

**No rule object this time.** F134.8 handed the layout a rule so class charts could keep the old lines. Here
nothing keeps the old drawing: all 45 pictures change in one step. So the new layout and drawing are built beside
the old ones, tested on their own, and the old ones are deleted in the step that switches.

**No ADR is added or changed.** ADR 0103's rules are about a flow's grid and connectors. ADR 0118, a diagram is
data our renderer draws, holds as it is.

## Steps

One commit each, test first, a stop after each.

1. **The plan**, its five pictures, the picked design in `F134-mockups/`, and its rows in the TODO, the work log
   and the plans' index. *Test:* none (docs).
2. **The design, for what the first mockup leaves open.** Design A drawn for: two rails in one gap, two cards
   feeding one, arrows both ways and up, a highlighted card in the middle of a picture, a heading, a card no
   link touches, a picture with no links, the longest titles, and the light look. Louis sees them before step 3.
   *Test:* none (docs).
3. **A link joins two neighbouring rows.** The reader refuses any other, with the reason. *Test:* a link that
   skips a row and one inside a row are each refused with their message, and all 45 pictures still read.
4. **The rails, from the links.** `CardRails` groups a gap's links: the cards above that feed the same cards
   below share a rail. Data in, data out, no places yet. *Test:* one card feeding three is one rail, two cards
   feeding one is one rail, two cards feeding different cards are two, and a link `up` stands on the rail of the
   gap it crosses.
5. **The layout.** `OverviewLayout` places the cards in the new measures and the rails between them: a
   headline's room, a card's room, a rail's height and span, each end on its card, and a step between two rails
   that would lie on one another. *Test:* every line fits its card by the measure, no two cards overlap, each
   rail's ends stand on its cards' edges, two overlapping rails differ in height, and a gap grows with its rails.
6. **The cards' style.** `CardsStyle` on the shared look, names that start `crd-`: the light, the rail and its
   glow, the glass card, the quiet card, the headline, the heading and the code pill, in both schemes. *Test:*
   the style names every class the drawing will use, and both schemes set every variable it reads.
7. **The drawing.** `OverviewDrawing` draws what the layout placed: the title, the light behind a highlighted
   card, the rails with their arrowheads and dots, and each card by its look. The renderer still draws the old
   way, so no docs picture changes. *Test:* a golden with every look and every kind of link; a headline has no
   box; a quiet card has no accent line; with no highlighted card there is no light.
8. **The switch.** `CardsRenderer` hands its picture to the new drawing, and the docs' frame gives a cards
   picture only its note bar. The old layout, its records, its tests and its goldens go. The 45 pictures are
   redrawn. *Test:* the docs' picture tests; no flow, class chart, sequence, ticket or component picture changed;
   every redrawn picture looked at on a sheet, light and dark.
9. **Documented and done.** *Draw a diagram* and its skill say how a cards picture is drawn now. The diagrams'
   design doc, the write-up and the work log follow. F134's plan says this child is built, and this plan is
   deleted.

## Approved during the build

- **2026-11-14, step 2: what the design drew.** Seven pictures in [m-cards.html](F134-mockups/m-cards.html),
  each from a real picture's JSON: the web's overview dark and light, the building blocks, F148's layers, the
  triage identity, the general packs and the site pack.
  - *One card over one card is a short lit bar*, the rail's glow with a node on it, as F148's chain shows.
  - *Dots run only where every link of a rail points down.* A link both ways has an arrowhead at each end and
    no dots, since dots would say one way.
  - *A long title on a narrow card goes under its badge*, on a line of its own, as a code name always does on a
    plain card.
  - *The measures:* a gap with a rail is 52 tall, one without 26, and a second rail's step is 18.
  - *The light look:* the same picture on white, the light a soft tint, the glass a faint slate, the dots in the
    accent.
  - *Not drawn, since no picture has one:* a link `up`, and two rails whose spans overlap. Step 5's tests hold
    both with numbers.
- **2026-11-14, step 3: the reader's check.** A link is read whichever way it is written, from the upper card
  or from the lower. The message names the link's two cards and says what a link may join. The check of a
  missing card comes first, so a misspelt id is still told as that.
- **2026-11-14, step 4: the rails.**
  - *Two cards that share only some of the cards they feed have a rail each.* The plan said the cards above that
    feed the same cards below share a rail. "The same" is taken exactly: on one rail, a card that feeds one of
    two would be drawn feeding both.
  - *A rail keeps its links as they were written*, so the layout can tell which ends an arrowhead belongs on.
  - *The order:* gap by gap from the top, and in a gap by the leftmost card above.
  - *Not yet used.* Nothing draws a rail before step 7, so the design doc gains only the folder's line.
- **2026-11-14, step 5: built as two commits.** 5a says how tall each kind of card is and where its words
  break, and 5b places the cards and stands the rails between them. One commit would have been too much to
  review in ten minutes.
- **2026-11-14, step 5a: the sizes.**
  - *`CardSizes`, in a folder of its own, `Cards/Sizes/`.* The namespace map put the measures in the layout.
    Three kinds of card, each with its own numbers, are a job of their own, and `Cards/` would have held seven
    files.
  - *The numbers are the design's.* A highlighted card with a code name and one line of text is 170 tall, a
    heading 92, a glass card with three lines 132, a quiet card with one line 74.
  - *A character's width is taken wide:* 8 for a glass card's title, 6.1 for a card's text, 6.6 for a headline's.
    Every line fits its card by that measure, at the widths a row of one, two, three or four cards gives.
