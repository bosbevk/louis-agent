# CL65.6: The engine asks its types

> **Plan.** A child of [CL65](CL65-the-diagrams-as-an-engine.md), built before CL65.4 and CL65.5. The diagram
> engine's sums move onto types that know them: a box, a point, a face for words and a path writer. It is the
> first code brought whole under [ADR 0123](../adr/0123-ask-the-type.md).

**Status:** approved 2026-11-14 with its parent (Louis: "approve go unattended"), and by his picks below.
**Branch:** `feature/F134.1-the-start-is-a-stage`.

## Context

Louis, 2026-11-14, of the engine: "with all these math in the egine, can we not have something like a calculator
which does it neatly." His picks since, each of three:

- Small types that carry their own sums, not one calculator class.
- Microsoft's `RectangleF` and `Vector2`, and then: "we should have an interface to save ours while we test the ms
  library", "our own will be Box<T> decision behind our interface".
- "CL 65.6 must be an ADR, we cannot write spaghetti code", which became ADR 0123, for all C#.
- "always fix touched files", so this child comes before the two that would touch the engine without its types.
- What the interface covers: a box and a point, made by one geometry. Words and paths are plain types of ours.
- Which family is in use, said while this plan was written: "keep ours and MS's but use ours.", "since we have
  it". So both stay behind the interface, and ours draws every picture.

**What was looked up.**

- *The engine today,* measured with both spellings of a size, `W` and `Width`: 134 files and about 4,900 lines of
  code.

  | What is worked out where it stands | How often |
  |---|---|
  | A box's right edge, `X + W` | 33, in 16 files |
  | A box's bottom edge, `Y + H` | 38, in 14 files |
  | Half a size, `W / 2` or `H / 2` | 39, in 19 files |
  | How wide words are, their length times a number | 20, in 8 files, with 11 different numbers |
  | A path, built as text | 20, in 13 files |

- *The boxes that have no type.* Nine records hold a place and a size as four numbers, some as `W` and `H` and
  some as `Width` and `Height`: a flow's box and its group, a card, a sequence's head and block, a component's
  box, neighbour, pill and key, and a ticket's pill. The routing has a tenth of its own, `Block`.
- *The points that have no type.* A point is an unnamed pair, `(double X, double Y)`, in six files, and a record,
  `LinePoint`, in six others.
- *Who reads a placed box outside the engine:* the web's `GraphView`, which draws a flow as a page's own markup,
  the docs' tool and its tests, and 246 lines in 25 of the diagrams' test files.
- *The pattern.* Abstract Factory (Refactoring.Guru): one factory makes a family of related things, so no code
  mixes a box of one family with a point of another. Its cost, as the page says, is more interfaces and classes.
- *How the repo hands a choice to a layout.* `GraphLayout.Place` takes its rules as a parameter with a default,
  `FlowRules? rules = null`. The geometry is handed in the same way.
- *Microsoft's types,* read on Microsoft Learn: `System.Drawing.RectangleF` has a box's edges and its sums, and
  `System.Numerics.Vector2` a point's, both in single precision. Both are in .NET's shared framework, so no
  package is added. Generic math (`INumber<T>`) lets a type of ours do its sums over any number type.
- *[ADR 0055](../adr/0055-keep-it-simple.md):* an interface with one implementation is a class. So a face and a
  path writer, which Microsoft has nothing to set beside, get no interface.

**What could not be confirmed** before the build:

- Whether a drawing ever needs the geometry itself, or only the boxes its layout made. Step 5 shows it.
- Whether our family in `double` redraws every picture to the same bytes. Each step from 5 to 9 proves it for the
  kinds it moves, and stops if a picture changes.
- How many pictures differ under Microsoft's family. Step 10 counts them.

## Design

**One geometry makes boxes and points.** `IGeometry` has two families behind it.

- *Ours:* `Box<T>` and `Point<T>`, generic over the number by .NET's generic math, made by
  `GenericGeometry<T>`. `double` is the type in use, so no number changes.
- *Microsoft's:* `DotNetBox` on `RectangleF` and `DotNetPoint` on `Vector2`, made by `DotNetGeometry`.

A layout is handed the geometry, as it is handed its rules, and ours is the default. A box makes its neighbours
itself: grown, moved, joined with another. So a whole picture is drawn by one family.

**A box knows its own sums:** its edges, its middle, and what the steps find a layout or a drawing asks of it. A
member is added by the step that first asks for it, never ahead (ADR 0123).

**A placed record holds a box,** where it held four numbers under two spellings, and a point where it held a pair.

**A face knows how wide words are.** The eleven numbers stay as they are, each named for the words it measures.

**A path writer writes a path,** where 20 places built its text by hand.

**A rule test holds it as a ratchet:** outside the geometry, the face and the path writer, the engine has no
record that is a box by four numbers, no words measured by their length, and no path built as text. Its list
starts with today's files and each step shortens it.

## Steps

One commit each, test first. Unattended. A step that proves too big to review is split, and said so below.

1. **The plan**, its four pictures and its row in the plans' index. *Test:* none (docs).
2. **Our family.** The three interfaces, `Box<T>`, `Point<T>` and their geometry. *Test:* each sum a box and a
   point have, in `double`.
3. **Microsoft's family**, on `RectangleF` and `Vector2`. *Test:* the same tests run over both families, and
   `Box<float>` against Microsoft's, which says what a difference is single precision's and what the library's.
4. **A face, a path writer, and the ratchet.** *Test:* a face's width of words for each of the eleven numbers; a
   path's text; the rule test, with every file that breaks it today on its list.
5. **The flow on its types**, the class chart's and the composite's adapters with it, and the web's `GraphView`.
   *Test:* the flow's own tests read a box; the docs' tool writes no picture; the web's goldens hold.
6. **The class chart's and the composite's drawings.** *Test:* as step 5, for their pictures.
7. **The cards and the sequence.** *Test:* as step 5.
8. **The components and the ticket.** *Test:* as step 5, and the site pack's tests for the ticket.
9. **What every kind shares:** the builder, the keys' placer, the frame, and the geometry handed in from
   `DiagramRenderers`. *Test:* the ratchet's list is empty.
10. **Both families draw every picture.** Ours is the one in use, and Microsoft's is kept, so a test draws each of
    the docs' pictures with Microsoft's too. *Test:* every picture is drawn by both without a refusal, and the
    ones that differ are counted and listed for the write-up.
11. **Documented and done.** The design doc, the write-up, the work log, the parent's plan, this plan deleted.

## Concepts

- Steps 2 and 3: Abstract Factory, two families behind one seam, and generic math, one type over any number.
  Skill 20 (extensibility).
- Step 3: what single precision does to a number, shown by running our own type in `float`. Skill 19 (measuring).
- Steps 4 to 9: Value Object as the cure for Primitive Obsession, and a ratchet whose list only shrinks. Skill 18
  (testing and shipping).
- Step 10: a second implementation kept behind a seam is only kept if something runs it.

## Approved during the build

- **2026-11-14, unattended from step 1,** with its parent. Louis: "approve go unattended."
- **2026-11-14, names picked while the plan was written, open for Louis to overrule.** The folders `Geometry`,
  `Geometry/Generic`, `Geometry/DotNet`, `Words` and `Svg/Paths`. Microsoft's family is `DotNet…`, since a
  namespace of ours named `Microsoft` would hide Microsoft's own. A placed record's box is its `Box`.
- **2026-11-14, step 2's picks, made unattended and open for Louis to overrule.**
  - *What a box can do from the start:* its edges, its middle, and grown, moved and joined, since the engine
    does each of those by hand today (a pad round a box and a frame round a group's boxes among them). A
    point has only its place until a step asks more of it.
  - *A box is a `sealed record`,* not a record struct. [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-records-for-values-classes-for-things.md)
    keeps a record struct for a hot path that was measured, and none was.
  - *The seam speaks `double`,* and a box keeps its own number type inside, so the sums run in that type.
- **2026-11-14, what step 3 measured.** Over 200 boxes whose numbers have no exact form, our box in `float`
  answers exactly what Microsoft's does, for every sum. So where Microsoft's differs from ours in `double`,
  all of it is single precision's doing and none the library's. It differs for most of those boxes, and by
  under a thousandth of a pixel.
