# F134.13: A composite structure diagram

> **Plan.** A child of [F134](F134-the-showroom-looks-modern.md): a new kind of picture, UML's composite structure
> diagram. It shows the inside of one class: the parts it is built from, the ports on its edge, and what joins
> them. It is drawn in the look every kind has now, and placed by the flow's layout.

**Status:** approved 2026-11-14 (Louis: "finish unattended, then refactoring"). **Branch:**
`feature/F134.1-the-start-is-a-stage`, where F134.7 to F134.12 were built. **Design:**
[m-composite.html](F134-mockups/m-composite.html), opened in a browser from the repo.

## Context

Louis, 2026-11-14: "134.12 first, then we need 134.13 composite diagram, then refactor". Of my first reading, that
it was the component diagram redone, he said "no do research", with UML's own line for it: "Illustrates the
internal parts, ports, and connectors of a structured class". Then he sent a page to read, with "our graph types
must match".

**What was looked up.**

- *What UML asks for.* Read: the page he sent (GeeksforGeeks' introduction to UML), Wikipedia, Visual Paradigm's
  guide and IBM's. The class is a frame with its name. A part is a box inside it, written `role : Type`, with how
  many beside it. A port is a small square on the frame's edge, with a name: what it offers is drawn as a ball,
  what it needs as a cup. A connector is a plain line between two parts, or between a port and a part. IBM's
  words for the difference: it is like a class diagram, "but it depicts individual parts instead of whole
  classes".
- *What we have.* The `components` kind draws one Blazor component, the boxes it holds inside one another and the
  pins on their edges. It has no lines between two boxes that stand side by side, and its layout stacks boxes
  top to bottom. A class chart has no layout of its own: `ClassFlow` turns it into a flow, and the flow's layout
  places it.
- *A real class to draw.* `AgentEngine` in `louis-agent.core`. Its constructor builds six parts and is handed
  three things from outside. The designs and the first docs picture are read from it.
- *The rules that will run.* The reader refuses a JSON field it doesn't know. `DocsDiagramsTests` holds each SVG
  to its JSON, and every flow and class chart to clear words and lines. A folder holds six source files at most.

**His picks.**

- *The design is C, "Pins"* (his whole answer: "C"), of three drawn on `AgentEngine`. A port is a named pin on
  the frame's edge. A part shows its type large and its role in a chip. Two parts that work together are joined
  by a plain line, and what is passed on runs with an arrow. I had recommended A, UML's squares, ball and cup.
- *The layout is the flow's, through an adapter* (his answer: "1"), of three: an adapter, a layout of its own, or
  growing the `components` kind.
- *"The type of diagram must always display."* A new rule, his words.

**Picked for him, unattended, and his to overrule.**

- *The type stands at the right of the title's line,* in the small quiet capitals a group's name has. Three
  places were drawn; the other two were a pill before the title and a line beside the key's heading.
- *The type shows on this kind now, through the shared start of a picture. The other kinds get it in the
  refactor.* That is where each picture's UML type is settled: a `flow` today draws several UML types, so its
  type can't come from the kind alone.

**What was proved before this plan.** The doubt in the adapter was whether a pin can sit on the frame's edge. It
was drawn as a plain flow, twice. With the ports outside the group, the layout puts them in a band of their own
and three lines cross the frame's top 18 apart, too close for three pins. With the ports inside the group, in
the first and last columns, the layout puts what is asked on the left, the parts in the middle and what is needed
on the right, every line clean. So the frame is drawn round the parts only, and each pin stands where its port's
line starts or ends. No line is cut.

**What could not be confirmed.** Two pages with UML's fine detail would not open (uml-diagrams.org, Sparx). So how
UML marks a line from a port to a part as passing a request on, and how it draws a port the class answers
itself, are not confirmed. Design C draws neither in UML's shapes, so nothing here rests on them.

## Design

**What the picture shows.** One class as a frame, with its name, a chip that says what it is, and where it lives.
Inside it, its parts. On its left edge the pins of what is asked of it, on its right edge the pins of what it
needs. Outside each pin, what is asked or needed in the code's own words: a method or an interface.

- *A part* is a panel with its type large, its role in a chip beside it, and a line of what it does.
- *A pin* is the component diagram's pin: a pill with an arrow and the port's name. The arrow points down for
  what is asked of the class, and up for what it needs.
- *A line from a pin to a part, or from a part to a pin,* is what is passed on. It has an arrowhead and the dots
  that run along a flow's line.
- *A line between two parts* says they work together. It is plain: no arrowhead and no dots. It may carry a word.
- *The key* stands under the faint rule: a pin each way, a plain line and a passed-on line.
- *The type,* "Composite structure diagram", stands at the right of the title's line.

**How it is placed: an Adapter onto the flow's layout.** `CompositeFlow` turns a composite into a flow. Every
part and every port is a box in one group. What is asked stands in the first column, the parts in the columns
after it by which part uses which, and what is needed in the last column. A box's label is what the layout
measures, so a part's label is its type and its role side by side, and a port's is its words and its pin's name.
The flow's layout then places it, and every routing fix a flow gets reaches this kind too.

**How it is drawn.** `CompositeDrawing` draws what the layout placed and decides no place itself, with one
exception: the frame. The layout's frame goes round every box, ports too. The drawing's frame goes round the
parts, and its left and right edges run through the middle of the pins.

**The JSON.** `"kind": "composite"`, and a `composite` part: the class's `name`, `kind`, `aside` and `tone`, its
`parts` (each an `id`, a `type`, a `role` and a `note`), its `ports` (each a `name`, a `side` of `in` or `out`,
the `part` it passes to or comes from, and a `label`), and its `connectors` (each `from` a part `to` a part it
uses, with a `word` if it has one).

**What is refused.** A composite with no part. Two parts with one id. A port or a connector that names a part
the class doesn't have.

**No ADR is added or changed.** A new rule in one needs Louis's word, and "the type of diagram must always
display" may become one in the refactor, when it reaches every kind.

## Steps

One commit each, test first. Unattended: no stop after a step, and every pick is recorded under *Approved during
the build*.

1. **The plan**, its four pictures, the designs in `F134-mockups/`, and its rows in the TODO, the work log and
   the plans' index. *Test:* none (docs).
2. **The kind, its records and its reader.** `"kind": "composite"` is read into `CompositeSpec`, and checked.
   *Test:* a composite is read with every field; no part, a doubled id, and a port or connector that names no
   part are each refused with their reason.
3. **The adapter.** `CompositeFlow.Of` turns a composite into a flow. *Test:* every part and port is a box in one
   group; what is asked stands in the first column and what is needed in the last; a part stands after the
   parts that use it; placed by the flow's layout, each pin's line starts or ends at its port.
4. **The type's name, in the shared start of a picture.** `PictureParts.Open` takes a type and writes it at the
   right of the title's line. A flow and a component diagram pass none yet. *Test:* a picture opened with a
   type shows it once, and one opened without shows none.
5. **The drawing, its style and its key, and the renderer.** `CompositeDrawing` draws the frame through the
   pins, the parts, the plain lines, the passed-on lines, the pins and their words; `CompositeKey` the key.
   *Test:* a golden; every word of the JSON is in the picture; a pin a port, down for `in` and up for `out`; a
   line between two parts has no arrowhead and no dots; the frame's edges run through the pins.
6. **The docs hold it, and the first real picture.** `DocsDiagramsTests` holds a composite to clear words and
   lines, through its flow. `AgentEngine` is drawn in core's design doc. *Test:* the docs' tests; the picture
   looked at, dark and light.
7. **Documented and done.** *Draw a diagram* and its skill, the diagrams' design doc, the write-up and the work
   log. F134's plan says this child is built, and this plan is deleted.

## Approved during the build

- **2026-11-14, unattended from step 1.** Louis: "finish unattended, then refactoring." No stop after a step;
  every pick is recorded here.
- **2026-11-14, step 2: the kind, its records and its reader.**
  - *A port has no id: its name is what it is known by,* since nothing else names a port.
  - *A connector is written from the part that uses the other.* The layout reads that as left to right, so what
    is asked stands before what does it, and that before what it uses.
  - *A pin's colour is its port's tone, and a port with none wears the class's.* The design drew what is asked
    in the class's green and what is needed in teal, so a picture's JSON says `teal` on those ports.
  - *The reader names which end of a connector is astray,* by saying which part named the missing one.
- **2026-11-14, step 3: the adapter.**
  - *The ports are in the class's group, in the first and last columns,* as the second proof showed. A port
    outside the group would be placed in a band of its own.
  - *A box's label is padded to the room its drawing needs,* since the layout measures a box by its label's
    length: a part's type large with its role's chip beside it, a port's words with its pin beside them. The
    drawing writes none of that label itself.
  - *A port's box has the id `port:` and its name,* so a port named as a part is told apart.
  - *It reads the component diagram's sizes for a chip and a pin,* since it draws that kind's chip and pin.
- **2026-11-14, step 4: the type's name, in the shared start of a picture.**
  - *The type is written by the shared start, when it is handed one,* in the class a group's name has, ending
    at the picture's right side on the title's line. A flow and a component diagram hand it none yet, so no
    picture changed: the tool wrote none.
  - *A picture is at least as wide as its title and its type side by side,* with room between them
    (`PictureParts.HeadWidth`), so the two never run into one another.
  - *The guide got its section for this kind in this step, not in step 7.* A rule test asks an example of
    every kind the reader knows (`EveryKind_HasItsExample`). It went red with step 2's commit and I had not
    run the docs' tests then: steps 2 and 3 were committed with it failing. The section says how to write a
    composite and what is refused; how it is drawn joins it in step 5.
- **2026-11-14, step 5: the drawing, its style and its key, and the renderer.**
  - *The renderer stands beside its drawing,* in `Svg/Renderers/Parts/Structure`. `Svg/Renderers` holds six
    files, its limit. Moving the six there is the refactor's work, and is on its list.
  - *The key's items are placed in the key's own class,* a fourth time the same few steps are written. Also
    on the refactor's list.
  - *The style has two rules of its own.* The rest is the other looks': a flow's word and rule, a component's
    pin and chip. A composite's file carries those kinds' styles, so it can't drift from them.
  - *A pin points down for what is asked and up for what is needed,* as the component diagram's pins do for
    what is handed down and what is said back up. So a pin that points up is hollow, as theirs is.

    circled: "line issue". A second line from the same part ran across the pill. A test holds the order.
  - *A part with no role has no chip, and a port with no label has no words outside its pin.*
  - *The bare drawing writes its caption as a flow's does.* The docs' frame replaces it with the note bar.
