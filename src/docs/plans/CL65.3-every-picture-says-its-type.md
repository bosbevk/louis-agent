# CL65.3: Every picture says its type

> **Plan.** The third child of [CL65](CL65-the-diagrams-as-an-engine.md). A picture's JSON states what type of
> diagram it is, by UML's name, and the picture shows it at the right of its title's line.

**Status:** approved 2026-11-14 with its parent (Louis: "approve go unattended"). **Branch:**
`feature/F134.1-the-start-is-a-stage`.

## Context

Louis, 2026-11-14: "our graph types must match", with GeeksforGeeks' introduction to UML, and "the type of
diagram must always display." His pick of three: a picture states its type. A kind stays a layout.

**What was looked up.**

- *UML's types,* from the page he sent. Structural: class, composite structure, object, component, deployment,
  package. Behavioural: state machine, activity, use case, sequence, communication, interaction overview. It
  names a timing diagram too, without describing it.
- *Our kinds against them.* A `sequence` is a sequence diagram, `classes` a class diagram, a `composite` a
  composite structure diagram. A `components` picture shows a composite structure too. `cards` and `ticket` are
  not UML. A `flow` draws several types.
- *The 93 flows.* Each was listed with its title, its shapes and its arrows' words. Five types cover them:
  activity, component, deployment, object and package. Which is which is a call a picture at a time, made in
  step 4 and listed there.
- *Where a type is shown today.* The builder writes it at the right of the title's line when a kind tells it
  one. Only the composite does. A picture is then at least as wide as its title and type side by side, except
  a cards picture and a component diagram, which are as wide as their body.
- *The reader* refuses a field it does not know, and reads the kind before anything else.

**What could not be confirmed** before the build: whether any cards picture or component diagram is narrower
than its title and type side by side. Step 3 counts them and says what was done.

## Design

**`DiagramType`.** An enum beside the kinds, in `louis-agent.diagrams`, with UML's thirteen names and one of our
own, `overview`. Its wire names are UML's words in lower case with hyphens: `class`, `composite-structure`,
`state-machine`, and so on.

**A source has a type.** `DiagramSource.Type`, written `"type"` in the JSON.

- A kind that is one type need not write it: a `sequence` is `sequence`, `classes` is `class`, a `composite`
  and a `components` picture are `composite-structure`, `cards` is `overview`. The reader fills it in.
- A `flow` must write its own, one of `activity`, `component`, `deployment`, `object`, `package`,
  `state-machine`, `use-case` or `communication`. The reader refuses a flow without one.
- A type that is not the kind's is refused: a `sequence` that says `class`, or a `flow` that says `sequence`.
- A `ticket` has none. It is a card in a visitor's chat.

**The picture shows it.** Each kind's drawing tells the builder its type's name: "Class diagram", "Activity
diagram", "Overview". The names are the engine's words, in `DrawingMessage`.

## Steps

One commit each, test first. Unattended.

1. **The plan**, its four pictures and its row in the plans' index. *Test:* none (docs).
2. **The type as data.** The enum, the field, the reader filling a kind's own type and refusing one that is not
   the kind's. A flow may still leave it out. *Test:* each kind's own type is filled in; a wrong one is refused
   with what the kind's types are; a flow keeps the one it wrote; no picture changes.
3. **Every kind shows its type.** The names, and each drawing telling the builder. A flow shows its type when it
   has one. *Test:* each kind's picture has its type's name once, at the right of the title's line; the goldens;
   every redrawn picture differs from before by that line and, where it grew, its width.
4. **Every flow says its type.** The 93 flows' JSON, each read and given one, and the reader refusing a flow
   without. *Test:* a flow without a type is refused; the docs' pictures are all read.
5. **Documented and done.** The guide and its skill, the design doc, the write-up, the work log, the parent's
   plan, this plan deleted.

## Approved during the build

- **2026-11-14, unattended from step 1,** with its parent. Louis: "approve go unattended."
- **2026-11-14, step 2's picks, made unattended and open for Louis to overrule.**
  - *Three refusals, where the plan said two.* A name that is no type, a type the kind can't be, and a third
    for a ticket that writes one: it has no type at all, and "its type may be:" followed by nothing says
    nothing. So it is told to take the field out.
  - *The check stands in its own file,* `Sources/Types/DiagramTypeCheck`, as the components' and the
    composite's checks do, and the reader calls it. The type's three holes records are in
    `Messages/Holes/Types`, since `Holes/Reading` would have held seven files.
  - *The writer leaves a kind's own type out,* as it leaves out everything at its default, and writes a
    flow's straight after the kind, where a person writes it.
- **2026-11-14, step 3's count and picks, made unattended and open for Louis to overrule.**
  - *The count this plan could not confirm.* No cards picture and no component diagram in the repo is
    narrower than its title and type side by side: 45 cards pictures and one component diagram, none.
  - *The builder's narrow step is gone.* It kept those two kinds as wide as their body whatever their title.
    With a type on the title's line that would let a type stand over a long title one day. Every picture is
    now as wide as its title and type at least. No picture changed by it.
  - *What changed.* 172 of the docs' pictures and four goldens were redrawn. 175 differ from before by the
    type's line and nothing else. One sequence, the order of a run's lines in `louis-agent.evals.protocol`,
    is also 53.6 wider, since its title and "Sequence diagram" need that.
  - *The names are in the engine's `Types/TypeNames`,* not in its `Messages` folder as the namespace map
    says. A rule test holds that a `Messages` folder has only message enums. The words themselves are
    fourteen members of `DrawingMessage`.
  - *A source built in code is named for its kind's own type,* since no reader filled it in.
- **2026-11-14, step 4's picks, made unattended and open for Louis to overrule.**
  - *The 95 flows, by type:* 54 component, 16 activity, 16 object, 6 package, 3 deployment. None is a state
    machine, a use case or a communication diagram.
  - *The rule each was held to.* Parts joined by calls or data, such as classes, services and endpoints: a
    component diagram. The steps of a procedure, with a start, an end or a decision: an activity diagram.
    Instances in memory: an object diagram. Sets of projects or files and what they depend on: a package
    diagram. What runs on which machine or cloud service: a deployment diagram. Ten were read box by box
    because their name and shapes pointed another way.
  - *The doubtful ones.*
    - `03-context-and-scope`: component. It shows people and outside systems round ours. UML's nearest is a
      use case diagram, but its lines are protocols, not what a person uses it for.
    - `louis-agent.site-how-it-runs`: component. It could be a deployment diagram, but its boxes are
      projects, not machines.
    - `ARCHITECTURE-the-build-and-the-verbs`: deployment. It could be an activity diagram of a deploy.
    - `puls-track-between-the-phases`: activity, as what comes after what. A roadmap is none of UML's types.
    - `claude-instructions-layers`, `README-how-it-s-built`, `tests-diagrams` and the `phase-10` picture:
      package, since their boxes are sets of files or projects.
    - The four project pictures titled *How it works* whose boxes are parts and not steps, core's, the API's
      and the two providers': component.
  - *A fourth refusal:* a flow that says no type is refused with the eight it may be.
  - *The docs' import from Mermaid writes `activity`,* which is what UML calls a flowchart. It can't tell
    what a chart shows, so a person changes it when the chart is another type.
  - *Six flows are wider,* by 14 to 157, for their title and type to stand side by side. Their body stands
    at the left, under the title. Whether a body narrower than its picture should stand in the middle is
    open.
