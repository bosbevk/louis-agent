# F134.1: The start is a stage

> **Plan.** The first child of [F134](F134-the-showroom-looks-modern.md): how the showroom's start becomes a stage,
> built from general parts the framework keeps for any project.

**Status:** approved 2026-11-13 (Louis: "go, commit the plan and start step 1"). **Branch:** `feature/F134.1-the-start-is-a-stage`.
**Mockup:** [f-centre-stage.html](F134-mockups/f-centre-stage.html), opened in a browser from the repo.

## Context

Today `/demos` is a feature card and a row of small flat covers, each with an emoji that Windows draws black and
uneven. Louis picked F, centre stage: one demo at a time as a window in the light, the other four behind it in
depth, a dock under it. His rule for building it: "things that can be shared, must go to framework", "like cool
components we could reuse later in other projects".

This child builds the start only. A demo's own page, the agent bar and what the agent does on the stage are the next
children. The one thing a demo's page gets here is its rail's line icons, since the emoji sign leaves the record.

## Design

**General parts, in the framework's web.** Each is named for what it is, knows nothing of demos, and has its own
test, so a later project uses it alone:

- **`LineIcon` and `LineIconView`** (`Components/Icons`). An enum (ADR 0024) with one inline SVG per value, drawn in
  the colour of the words around it, at any size. The paths are **[Tabler Icons](https://tabler.io/icons)** (MIT,
  Louis's pick of 2026-11-11), copied in, with no package and no icon font, and one line in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES`.
- **`LitStage`** (`Components/Staging`). A light from above, a pool of it on the floor and the floor's lines, in one
  accent colour, around whatever it holds. Styles only.
- **`Deck<TItem>`** (`Components/Staging/Decks`). Draws each item through the caller's template: the one forward in
  the middle, the rest at their distance behind it, with arrows and the arrow keys. `DeckPlace` is the plain rule
  for the distance, the short way round. Which item is forward comes in as a parameter and goes out as an
  `EventCallback`, so the page owns it.
- **`FramedWindow`** (`Components/Staging/Windows`) and its two bodies. The frame is a browser's bar with an address
  word, then a body. `SiteBody` is a website with its chat: a photo, a name and a line, then a question, an answer
  and one thing the agent showed. `WorkBody` is panes of work, each a heading over a few lines.
- **`Dock<TItem>`** (`Components/Staging/Docks`). A button per item with its icon, a divider between groups, and the
  current one showing its name. Each button is a card of the page (its id, `data-card`, `aria-expanded`), so the
  framework's addresses open it with no new script.

**The showroom puts them together.**

- `ShowroomDemo` loses `Sign` and gains `LineIcon Icon` and `Type? Window`: the component that fills its window, the
  way a `SiteArea` names its admin card. A demo with none gets `PlainBody`, its icon large over its name and line.
- `ShowroomStart` becomes: the `LitStage` in the forward demo's colour, the page's heading and lede, the `Deck` of
  the site's demos (each a `FramedWindow` around its body), the forward demo's name, line and way in, and the `Dock`.
  `Featured` stays, as the demo that is forward first. `Kicker`, `Play` and `Preview` leave with the old feature.
- `ShowroomShell` draws no rail and no chips when no demo is on screen. On a demo's page they stay.
- On a phone the deck is a row swiped one window at a time, with no 3D. The deck's script module only tells the page
  which window is in view, and leans the stage towards the cursor on a wide screen.

**The site fills five windows.** `louis-agent.site` gets one small component per demo, built from the two bodies,
and `SiteWindowTalks`: for each business, the question, the answer and the recording they come from. A test reads
the recordings and fails when a window's words are not in its own.

**The patterns.** *Templated components* (Microsoft's Blazor guidance): the deck and the dock draw any item through
the caller's fragment. *A slot named by its type*, as `SiteArea.AdminCard` already is. *Carousel* (the WAI-ARIA
Authoring Practices): one item in view, a control before and after, the keyboard's arrows. *Presentation rule as a
plain class*: `DeckPlace` is tested with no component. Each class that carries one names it in its `<remarks>`.

**Two things a step has to confirm**, said here so they are not a surprise:

- The script that opens a card from its address is the site shell's. Step 7 confirms it runs in the showroom's
  frame. If it does not, that step gives the showroom's frame the same call, with a test.
- The way in under the deck says "Watch this demo", from the showroom's words. The mockup says "Watch the
  guesthouse", which would need a word per demo. Say so if the per-demo word matters.

## Steps

Each step is one commit with its test, written first and seen failing. Stop after each for Louis's review.

1. **Line icons.** `LineIcon` and `LineIconView`. `ShowroomDemo.Sign` becomes `Icon`. The rail and the start's covers
   draw the view, and the chips' dot becomes it. `SiteDemos` names the five, and the notice gets its line.
   *Test:* every `LineIcon` draws an `svg` with a path and `aria-hidden`. The rail holds an `svg` and no text sign.
2. **The lit stage.** `LitStage`, in an accent, around its content. *Test:* it wears the accent's class and holds
   its content. Its styles write no colour of their own and stand still for a visitor who asked for less motion.
3. **The deck.** `DeckPlace`, then `Deck<TItem>`. *Test:* with five items and the first forward, the distances are
   0, 1, 2, -2, -1. The deck marks one item forward, an arrow moves it and wraps, and the arrow keys do the same.
4. **The framed window and its two bodies.** *Test:* the frame holds its address word and its body. `SiteBody` has
   its photo with an empty `alt`, its question and its answer. `WorkBody` has a heading per pane.
5. **The dock.** *Test:* a button per item with its icon, a divider between groups, the current one named and
   `aria-expanded`, each with its id and `data-card`.
6. **The start is the stage.** `ShowroomDemo.Window` and `PlainBody`. `ShowroomStart` is rebuilt from the parts. The
   shell draws no rail on the start. *Test:* the start holds one window per demo, the featured one forward, a demo
   with no window gets the plain body, and the start has no rail while a demo's page keeps its own. The site's
   golden of `/demos` is regenerated and read.
7. **Each window has its address.** `/demos/restaurant-demo` opens the start with the restaurant forward, and the
   address follows a press on the dock or an arrow. *Test:* opened at that address, the restaurant is forward. Every
   demo's place is in the page's list the guide reads.
8. **On a phone.** The deck is a swiped row, the dock under it. *Test:* the phone's rules hold the row and no 3D.
   Checked by eye at 390 px, since no test swipes.
9. **The site fills its five windows.** Five components, `SiteWindowTalks`, and `SiteDemos` naming each window.
   *Test:* each business window's question and answer are in its recording, word for word.
10. **By eye, docs and done.** `/demos` at an exact 1920×1080 and at 390 px, every window forward in turn. ADR 0115
    gains the stage and the general parts. The web's and the site's design docs and their pictures follow. The work
    log, the write-up, and this plan deleted. *Test:* none (docs).

## Verification

- `louis-agent.web.tests` and `louis-agent.site.tests` per step, `core.tests` when a step adds folders or names,
  `puls.tests`, `code-review.tests` and `starter.tests` at step 1 and step 6, since every site's showroom record and
  frame change there. The full suite once after step 9.
- By eye in Chrome at both widths. No paid run: nothing here calls a model.

## Concepts

Skill 18 (testing and shipping): a general part proved alone by a markup test before anything is built from it, and
a rule (`DeckPlace`) kept out of the component so it is tested with no browser. Skill 20 (extensibility): a slot a
site fills by naming a type.
