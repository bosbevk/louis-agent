## Context

Louis's picks 2 to 5 (in the parent): a decision is given as **option cards** through a tool, `Offer`, a click is
the visitor's answer; a booking request is a **read-only card the agent draws with our diagram tool**; the look is the
design canvas's guesthouse board ([the canvas](https://claude.ai/artifact/6U2hR349TuHdTz9pa2xEV7)): cards with a
label, a one-line why, a *Suggested* pill and a chevron; a ticket with a band (kicker, title, subtitle, a status pill),
a torn edge, rows with an icon each and a note.

Today a showroom business repeats a booking back in words and sends it with `SendLead` on a typed yes; the guide
offers choices in prose. `louis-agent.diagrams` has four kinds and 24 icons, none for dates or a dog.

## Design

### What changes, in plain words

- **A ticket is a diagram:** a fifth kind, `ticket`, in `louis-agent.diagrams`: a kicker, a title, an optional
  subtitle and status, two to six rows (an icon, a label, a value) and an optional note, laid out and drawn as SVG by
  the library, its colours CSS variables so a business's window can wear its brand.
- **The agent draws it:** `ShowCard` in the site pack takes the ticket's parts, builds the diagram, checks it with
  the library's reader and answers *shown* or the reason it was refused, so the model can fix it.
- **The agent offers choices:** `Offer(question, choices)` in the site pack: two to four choices, a label each, an
  optional one-line why, one may be suggested; checked in code.
- **The widget draws both**, in the guide's widget and a business's window alike, with the work hidden too: the
  ticket as the library's SVG, the choices as buttons; a click sends the label as the visitor's message; once the
  visitor has answered (by a click or by typing), the cards fold to the one chosen.
- **The prompts:** the site toolset's guide says when to offer and when to show a card; each business's voice books
  by showing the request as a ticket and offering *Book it*, *Change something*, *Not now*, and sends the lead only
  on *Book it*, then shows the ticket again as *Sent*.

## Steps

One commit each, test first, seen failing, a stop after each.

1. **The plans**, the parent's and this one, their pictures drawn.
2. **The ticket's source.** `DiagramKind.Ticket`, the records; the reader refuses no rows, more than six, an empty
   title, and an unknown icon, each with its reason. Tests: read, refused, written back the same.
3. **The icons a ticket needs:** calendar, paw, clock, phone, home, tag (Tabler, MIT, its notice kept). Tests: each
   drawn.
4. **The ticket drawn.** `TicketLayout` and `TicketRenderer`: the band, the status pill, the torn edge, the rows, the
   note, colours as CSS variables with the site's own as fallbacks; a golden. Tests with numbers: every word inside.
5. **`ShowCard`** in the site pack: its parts into a `DiagramSource`, checked by the reader; *shown* or the reason.
   The pack references `louis-agent.diagrams` (plain C#, no engine). Tests.
6. **`Offer`** in the site pack: two to four choices, labels up to 40 characters, a why up to 80, at most one
   suggested; *offered* or the reason. Tests.
7. **The widget draws a card.** `CardCall` reads the block, `DrawnCard` draws the library's SVG in `BlocksView`, with
   the work hidden too. bUnit tests and their goldens.
8. **The widget offers.** `OfferCall`, `OfferCards`: buttons in the chosen look, a click sends the label through the
   runner, and the cards fold once the visitor has answered. bUnit tests.
9. **The prompts.** The site toolset's guide (when to offer, when to show a card, never to send before a click); the
   three businesses' voices book through a ticket and *Book it*; the guide offers instead of listing choices. The
   golden files regenerated and their diff read; a live check on the PC (Seemeeu books, the guide offers).
10. **The docs:** the diagrams', the web's and the site pack's design docs (their four pictures updated), Draw a
    diagram's `ticket` section, the work log; the full suite; this plan deleted.
