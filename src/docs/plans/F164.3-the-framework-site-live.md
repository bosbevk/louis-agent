A plan, F164's third and last child: the framework's site goes live on its own subdomain as a second Azure app,
released by the same workflow as louis-agent.dev, and louis-agent.dev links to it from its menu and its CV.

**What was looked up.** The repo: `deploy.ps1` knows one app in its `$Apps` table (image, Dockerfile, config folder,
yaml, the Bicep output naming its identity, share, hostname, the suffix on its own vault secrets); its `update`
creates an app that doesn't exist yet; its `domain` verb treats any name but `www.` as an apex, so a subdomain would
get the apex's A record. The Bicep build can't be run again: since FX122 it would recreate the VM and SQL server.
`deploy-puls.yml` releases `louis-agent-app` on every push to `main`, trusted from `main` only. The framework site's
`sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` already name framework.louis-agent.dev. `ResumeStore` reads a saved `cv.json` on the
share before the built-in one. `DeployFilesTests` and `DeployWorkflowTests` hold the deploy files; `SiteLinksTests`
holds every internal link of louis-agent.dev to a place.

Microsoft's docs: one user-assigned identity "can be used by multiple resources"
([managed identities](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/managed-identities-azure-resources/overview));
several container apps can mount one Azure Files share, each with its own `subPath`
([storage mounts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/container-apps/storage-mounts)).

**Not confirmed.** A subdomain's managed certificate through the `domain` verb: the verb's `www` branch (a CNAME and an
`asuid` TXT) is what a subdomain needs, but it has never bound anything but the apex here. Step 9 proves it live.
Whether a saved CV exists on the share is unknown until step 9 reads it.

## Design

### The shape, in plain words

**A second app, from the same table.** `deploy.ps1`'s `$Apps` gains `louis-agent-framework-app`: the image
`louis-agent-site-framework` from `Dockerfile.framework`, the yaml `src/site/deploy/azure/louis-agent-framework-app.yaml`,
louis-agent.dev's identity and share (pick 1), the hostname `framework.louis-agent.dev`, and the suffix `-framework` on
its own vault secrets. Its two keys, `AGENT_API_KEY` and `ADMIN_API_KEY`, come from a git-ignored file of their own,
`src/site/config/.env.secrets.framework`, since both apps read the site's config folder; the Anthropic key is shared.

**Its yaml mirrors louis-agent.dev's**, with the framework's settings: `OPEN_IDENTITIES=louis-agent-framework`, 20
sessions a day (pick 4), `DOCS_ROOT=/app/docs`, the engine's pacing, no `SITE_DATA_DIRECTORY`, no lead mail and no
peers, its logs on the share's `framework/logs`, half a CPU, asleep when nobody visits.

**A subdomain, not an apex.** The `domain` verb learns a subdomain: a CNAME from its first label to the app's own
address and a TXT at `asuid.` plus that label, the shape it already uses for `www`.

**One workflow, two apps.** `deploy-puls.yml` releases both apps on every merge, one after the other.

**louis-agent.dev links to it.** "Framework" in the menu, between Demos and Theme (pick 3), and the CV's four cards to
the framework site's places: The engine to `/how-it-works`, Evals in C# to `/cost-and-evals/the-evals`, Every request
measured to `/cost-and-evals/the-ledger`, In production to `/overview/the-whole`.

## Steps

Each is one commit with its test, written first and seen failing, and a stop after each for Louis's review.

1. **The script knows the framework app.** `$Apps`' second entry, its own secrets file and its example, the
   `ValidateSet`. *Test:* `DeployFilesTests` holds the entry's image, Dockerfile, yaml, share, hostname and suffix.
2. **The domain verb knows a subdomain.** *Test:* the verb's records for `framework.` are a CNAME and an
   `asuid.framework` TXT, and the apex keeps its own.
3. **The framework app's yaml.** *Test:* it names its image, its own keys, the shared identity and share with logs in
   `framework/logs`, the door to `louis-agent-framework` at 20, `DOCS_ROOT`, and no VM, database or lead mail.
4. **The workflow releases both.** *Test:* `DeployWorkflowTests` holds a release step and a summary for each app.
5. **20 sessions a day everywhere.** *Test:* `FrameworkImageTests` holds the compose service and the run profile to 20.
6. **"Framework" on louis-agent.dev's menu.** *Test:* the menu links to `https://framework.louis-agent.dev` between
   Demos and Theme.
7. **The CV's four links.** *Test:* each card leads to its place on framework.louis-agent.dev, and that place is in
   the framework site's sitemap.
8. **The docs, the write-up, the records.** The deploy README's how-to for the second app and a subdomain, the
   framework site's and louis-agent.dev's design docs, the Azure architecture page, the write-up, the plan folded.
   Then `main` merged, the full suite, and #403's body, for Louis's merge.
9. **Live, after Louis's merge** (no commit unless something needs fixing). Louis puts the framework's keys in the
   vault with `deploy.ps1 secrets -App louis-agent-framework-app` before the merge. The merge releases both apps;
   `deploy.ps1 domain -App louis-agent-framework-app` prints the records; Louis adds them at Cloudflare, DNS-only; the
   domain verb runs again and binds the certificate. The saved `cv.json` on the share is read and shown to Louis, and
   deleted only on his yes then (pick 5). Every page is checked live, prerendered, at 1280 and 390, and Orbi is asked
   once, its cost reported.

## Verification

The touched tests per step; before the merge, `main` merged and the full unit suite. Free checks by hand: the yaml
`deploy.ps1 update` would write, printed without running it. Live, after the merge: each of the seven pages and
their places answer on framework.louis-agent.dev, drawn for a crawler; louis-agent.dev's menu and CV lead there; one
paid Orbi call, its cost reported (Louis, 2026-11-09: a test call of an agent needs no ask). Running a second app
costs Azure money while someone visits it; it sleeps at no replicas otherwise, as louis-agent.dev does.
