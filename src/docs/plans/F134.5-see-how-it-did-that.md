# F134.5: See how it did that

> **Plan.** The fifth child of [F134](F134-the-showroom-looks-modern.md). Since F134.4 the guide's answer stands as a
> caption beside the place it lit. This child lets the visitor turn that caption over and see how the answer was
> made: the tools it called, how long it thought, the tokens it used and what it cost, the same numbers the usage
> ledger holds. The bar carries the talk's total, and a demo's notes become pieces the visitor can ask about.

**Status:** built unattended on Louis's word of 2026-11-15: "Finish the whole F134 unattended, stop and show before
merge. want to check locally". The look is the J and K mockups', which he saw with F134's design.
**Branch:** `feature/F134.1-the-start-is-a-stage`, where F134 is being finished.

## Context

F134's design: "Any answer turns over to show what it called and what it cost." Its done-when for this child:
every answer of a live walk turns over to numbers that match the usage ledger. Its concepts: skill 13 (cost and
token economics) and skill 17 (observability), the framework's own records of an answer, shown to the person who
asked.

**What was looked up.**

- *The numbers are already there.* Each turn carries its `Usage`, a `TurnUsage` the API sends in `message_delta`:
  the four token counts summed over the turn's requests, how many requests, and the cost priced from the ledger, or
  none when a request had no price. `Chat.Usage()` sums the turns. `UsageFormat` writes a line
  (`1.9k in · 26k cached · 412 out · R0.066`), its exact figures for a tooltip, money in the visitor's currency, and
  the chat's total. The talk already prints the line under each answer.
- *The tools and the time.* A turn's blocks hold each tool call by its name and input, and a thinking block keeps
  how long it thought (`ThinkingSeconds`). `ShowPageCall.PathOf` and `OfferCall.Of` read the two tools the guide
  uses most.
- *Pieces.* A tile and a card deck are pieces already (F127.7): an element with an id and `data-piece`, which the
  widget's `PieceWatch` rings and hands to the widget, and the widget asks the guide about it at once. In the bar
  layout a pick is still sent, but the chip that shows the selected piece is drawn only in the corner's panel. No
  showroom part is a piece yet.
- *The mockups* (`F134-mockups/agent.js`): the caption's back is "How I did that", a list of the calls, then the
  time, the tokens and the cost, with "Back to the answer". The bar has a button with the talk's cost and its count
  of answers, which opens the drawer, and the drawer ends with the talk's total. A part with a dashed ring can be
  asked about.

## Design

**Turned over.** The caption gets a second face, "How I did that". It lists the tools the answer called: a shown
place by its address, an offer by how many choices it gave, any other tool by its name. Under them stand how long
the answer thought, its usage line, and the exact figures in the line's tooltip. The front carries the answer's cost
beside the guide's name. A button turns it over, and another turns it back. What the back shows is asked of a record
beside the caption, `AnswerWork.Of(turn)`, so the caption only draws it.

**The talk's total.** The bar carries a small button with the talk's cost so far and how many answers it has, read
from `Chat.Usage()`, and it opens the whole talk. The drawer's foot says the same total.

**Pieces.** On a demo's page each note of "a custom agent could also" is a piece, as a tile is. The bar shows the
selected piece's chip and its close, as the corner does.

## Steps

One commit each, test first. Unattended.

1. **The plan**, its four pictures and its rows. *Test:* none (docs).
2. **What an answer did.** `AnswerWork.Of(turn)`: its tools, how long it thought, its usage. *Test:* a turn's
   tools, time and usage are read, a shown place by its address and an offer by its count.
3. **The caption turns over.** The back face and its buttons; the answer's cost on the front. *Test:* turned over,
   the caption shows the tools and the usage line; turned back, the answer.
4. **The talk's total.** The bar's button and the drawer's foot. *Test:* the button shows the chat's total and its
   answers, and opens the talk.
5. **The piece's chip in the bar.** *Test:* a selected piece shows its chip in the bar, and its close lets it go.
6. **A demo's notes are pieces.** *Test:* each note has a piece's id and mark.
7. **Checked live, docs and done.** A walk by the real guide, each answer's back held to the ledger's rows for its
   requests, its cost reported. The web's design doc, the write-up, the work log, this plan deleted.
