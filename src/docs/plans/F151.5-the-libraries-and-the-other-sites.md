# F151.5 The libraries and the other sites

A plan: how the account pages, the eval studio and the packs' views come to wear a theme, and what the three
other sites on the framework do: the starter, code-review and Puls. It is the fifth child of
[F151 Two themes, each light and dark](F151-themes-and-styles.md), after
[F151.4](F151.4-the-frameworks-components.md). It has nine steps, each with its own picture.

## Context

After F151.4 every component of the framework's web draws its surfaces through the shared tool. The libraries
above it do not yet: the accounts, the eval studio and the packs' views each have stylesheets of their own.
Louis's words are "the themes must cater for all our current components", and his pick for the other sites was
that every site gets the themes, with code-review's own picker replaced by the framework's and retro kept as that
demo's third theme, and Puls keeping its brand on top and only having to build and stay green.

The sizes, counted the same way as in F151.4: the accounts have 6 sheets with 16 lines that draw a surface, the
eval studio 71 with 191, and the packs' views 3 with 42. None of them names a raw colour. Code-review has a
picker, `ThemeSwitcher`, a script, `theme.js`, a list, `themes.json`, and two theme sheets, `console.scss` and
`retro.scss`, held by `ThemeTests`. Puls has its own tokens and 13 sheets, and `SiteColoursTests` makes it set
every name the framework does.

The starter names the accounts and the evals, so the six account routes and the five eval routes are looked at
there.

## Design

### 1. The libraries move to the tool

Nothing new is designed for the libraries. Their sheets include the surface tool where they draw a pane, a bar,
a press, a chip, a tag or a well by hand, and the rule test from F151.4 now reads their sheets too. Its list
grows by the libraries' sheets in step 2 and shrinks to nothing again by step 6.

![The libraries' sheets move to the surface tool, and code-review's retro becomes a theme like any other while its own picker goes](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-interface-design.svg)

### 2. The three other sites

![Three libraries draw through the tool, and three sites take their place](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-architecture.svg)

The starter is done since F151.2. Code-review names glass and flat, and retro becomes a theme by the same rule
as any other: an area that carries a theme, and a sheet of values. Its own picker, script and list are deleted,
since the framework's switch offers every theme a site names.

![Code-review names three themes, and the framework's switch offers them in the chat's settings](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-communication-code-review-opens-and-a-person-picks-retro.svg)

![Three themes in the order named, each held by its own area](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-objects-in-memory.svg)

Puls names no theme and shows no switch. It keeps the look it has, by setting each name after the framework's as
it always did, and it gains a value for each new surface name.

## Steps

Each step is one commit with its test, written first and seen failing on an assertion. Stop after each for
Louis's review.

### Step 1: the plan is kept

![This plan and its pictures](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-1-the-plan-is-kept.svg)

A docs-only step. This plan and its pictures are committed, and its row in the plans' index says it is approved.

### Step 2: the account pages

![Six sheets and six routes](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-2-the-account-pages.svg)

The test comes first. The rule test from F151.4 is widened to read the three libraries' sheets, and its list
gains each of their sheets that draws a surface by hand. It fails until the list is right.

Then the accounts' six sheets move to the tool and leave the list. The sign-in providers' buttons keep the
providers' own colours, which are theirs and not a theme's. By eye, on the starter: sign in, register, the
profile, confirm an address, forgot a password and reset one, each in all four looks.

### Step 3: the eval studio's overview

![The page a person lands on](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-3-the-studios-overview.svg)

The sheets of the studio's first page: the evals page itself, the summary, the charts, the trends and the rates.
A chart reads the colour names, which every theme sets, so its colours follow with no change of its own. Look
at each chart in both styles all the same, since a chart's contrast is the first thing a light style gets wrong.
By eye: `/admin/evals` in all four looks.

### Step 4: the runs

![What a run looks like](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-4-the-runs.svg)

The explorer, the list of runs, a run's page and a run that is live. By eye: a run's page in all four looks, with
a conversation opened in the explorer.

### Step 5: reviewing and building

![The rest of the studio](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-5-reviewing-and-building.svg)

The reviews, the studio where an eval is built, the prompts, and the single sheets left. By eye: `/admin/review`,
`/admin/studio` and `/admin/prompts` in all four looks. The studio's sheets are then off the list.

### Step 6: the packs' views

![Three sheets, 42 lines](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-6-the-packs-views.svg)

The triage pack's shared sheet, which louis-agent.dev's triage page reads, and the two other packs' screens. The
list is empty again. The triage page itself is looked at in F151.6, where louis-agent.dev wears a theme.

### Step 7: code-review takes the themes

![Code-review names glass, flat and retro, and its own picker goes](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-7-code-review-takes-the-themes.svg)

**A choice for Louis before this step.** A theme sets every name for both styles, and retro has only a dark
look: soft mint on dark green-grey. Put it to him with three options and wait: retro gets a light style of its
own, drawn first as a page he can open; retro offers one style, which changes the rule in ADR 0124 and so needs
his word twice; or retro is dropped and code-review has glass and flat.

The test comes first. `ThemeTests` is rewritten: code-review's areas list its themes in order, and each theme's
sheet leaves no name unset, by `ThemeSheet`.

Then the demo. Its areas name `.Glass().Flat()` and, on his pick, `.Retro()`. `RetroTheme` is an area that carries
the theme, in the demo's own project, since it is this demo's and no other site's. Its sheet moves under the
selector for its theme and style. `ThemeSwitcher`, `theme.js`, `themes.json` and `console.scss` are deleted, and
the chat page no longer puts a picker in the settings. The demo's `index.html` says its default theme. Its golden
pages change, since the settings now hold the framework's switch.

### Step 8: Puls keeps its brand

![Puls names no theme, sets each new name, builds, and its tests pass](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-8-puls-keeps-its-brand.svg)

`SiteColoursTests` is the test, and it is not changed: it asks Puls for every name the framework sets. Puls gets
a value for each surface name that keeps the look it has. Puls is on pause, so nothing else of it is touched: no
eval of it is run and no page of it is redesigned. It builds and its tests pass.

### Step 9: the docs, and the pull request

![The design docs of what changed, the full suite and the pull request](diagrams/F151.5-the-libraries-and-the-other-sites-step-9-the-docs-and-the-pull-request.svg)

The accounts' and the eval studio's design docs, the packs' views', and code-review's, each read against the
code as it now is. `main` is merged in, the full unit suite passes, and the pull request is opened on Louis's
word. Code-review is not deployed, and louis-agent.dev names no theme yet, so nothing live changes.

## Verification

The tests named in each step, then the full unit suite. By eye: the starter's eleven account and eval routes in
all four looks, and code-review on this PC with each of its themes. Puls builds. No run that spends money.

## Concepts

Step 7 shows what a plug-in point is worth: a third theme joins by the same door as the first two, and the
demo's own way of doing it is deleted. That is extensibility, skill 20 in [the goal](../GOAL.md).
